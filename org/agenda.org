1) What is an abstraction? Can you give an example (life, math or
   code)? What's the price to be paid for "abstracting"?
   #+begin_quote
   - *Life*: Calling Jim, Joe, John and Jane "students" is an abstraction in
     real life. It suppresses the details of their persons in order to
     be to address them with one term.
    
   - *Math*: An integer is a mathematical abstraction. It captures the idea of
     whole numbers ignoring their individual differences.

   - *Code:* The ~int~ data type is a computing abstraction of an integer
     number.

   - *Price*: Abstractions remove detail thereby removing whatever
     they're used for further from the real world of complexity.
   #+end_quote

10) What's the meaning of =std::cout=? Do you have to use it?
    #+begin_quote
    - This is actually quite complicated! The statement =std::cout=
      consists of:
      1) ~std~ for the standard C++ ~namespace~ (think of it as a library of
         useful functions).
      2) ~::~ the *scope resolution operator* which tells the compiler where
         to look for names (like ~cout~).
      3) ~cout~ is the *character output stream object* for console (stdout)
         output.
    - So the 1st answer is: =std::cout= is the standard output stream
      object from the std namespace.
    - Do you have to use it? No, you can use this instead: =using
      namespace std; cout << "Hello";= - It's in the OneCompiler.com/cpp
      template.
    #+end_quote
